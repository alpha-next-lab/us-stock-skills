# Analysis Framework

## Required Sections

1. Investment conclusion: rating-style view, time horizon, expected return range, confidence, and top 3 reasons.
2. Business quality: revenue model, segment mix, moat, customer concentration, unit economics, cyclicality, and operating leverage.
3. Industry structure: TAM, growth, competition, regulation, supply chain, and bargaining power.
4. Financial quality: growth, margins, cash conversion, ROIC/ROE, leverage, dilution, buybacks/dividends, and accounting red flags.
5. Earnings and estimates: recent quarter, guidance, consensus revisions, beat/miss quality, and next 1-3 catalysts.
6. Valuation: historical range, peer comps, forward multiples, DCF or reverse DCF when useful, and scenario-weighted target price.
7. Technical and positioning: trend, moving averages, support/resistance, relative strength, volume, options/short interest if available.
8. Risks and kill criteria: what breaks the thesis, indicators to monitor, and probability/severity.

## Institutional Lens

Use these questions to sharpen the analysis:

- What does consensus believe, and what is the differentiated view?
- Is the debate about revenue growth, margins, multiple, balance sheet, regulation, or capital allocation?
- Which KPI will move estimates next?
- Are expectations too high or too low into the next catalyst?
- What is the cleanest peer set and why?
- What would make a portfolio manager buy now instead of waiting?

## Valuation Method

Use at least two valuation approaches for important calls:

- Forward multiple: choose P/E, EV/EBITDA, EV/Sales, P/FCF, or NAV based on sector economics.
- Peer comp: compare against direct peers and explain premium/discount.
- Historical range: compare current multiple against 3-5 year range, adjusted for rate regime and growth.
- DCF/reverse DCF: use when long-duration growth, margins, or terminal assumptions drive the stock.
- Sum-of-the-parts: use for conglomerates, multi-segment platforms, or businesses with sharply different margins/growth.

Scenario structure:

- Bull case: upside drivers, target multiple/key assumptions, target price.
- Base case: most likely path, target price.
- Bear case: downside drivers, target price.
- Probability-weighted value and implied upside/downside.

## Quality Checks

Before finalizing:

- Reconcile market cap, enterprise value, net cash/debt, share count, and currency.
- Check whether latest quarter or guidance changed the denominator in valuation.
- Avoid mixing calendar year, fiscal year, and NTM estimates without labeling.
- Flag one-off items, SBC, restructuring, mark-to-market gains/losses, and acquisition effects.
- State whether analysis uses GAAP, non-GAAP, or adjusted metrics.
